    How the score works. Every 10 minutes of the day gets a score from 0 to 100 for crab snaring. It weighs how fast the water is moving (snares drag in strong current), how high the water is (high water brings crabs within casting range), the light (crabs feed most at dawn and dusk), the season, and, when forecasts are available, surf at spots open to the ocean, wind for casting, rain, and muddy water after heavy rain. The best window is the strongest two hours between first light and an hour after sunset. Full methodology and verification.
    Tides are computed from NOAA harmonic constants (via the open Neaps tide database), in feet above MLLW and in each spot's local time. Live wind and surf are from Open-Meteo; water temperature and observed water level are from NOAA Tides & Currents. Predictions can't see storms or river flow. Not for navigation.
    Check CDFW before you go. Rules here follow 14 CCR §29.80, §29.85 and §632 and CDFW's in-season notices as checked on Nov 5, 2026: Dungeness opens the first Saturday in November and closes June 30 (July 30 in Del Norte, Humboldt and Mendocino), never in San Francisco or San Pablo Bay; rock crabs are open all year. CDFW can delay or close the season (for example for domoic acid) at any time. Protected-area boundaries shown are approximate.
