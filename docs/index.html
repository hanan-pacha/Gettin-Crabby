    How the score works. Every 10 minutes of the day gets a score from 0 to 100. It weighs the tide (which way the water is moving and how fast), the light (crabs feed more at dawn and dusk), how big the day's tides are compared with normal for that spot, how active crabs usually are that month, and wind and rain when a forecast is available. The best window is the strongest two hours between first light and an hour after sunset.
    Tides are computed from NOAA harmonic constants (via the open Neaps tide database), in feet above MLLW and in each spot's local time. Live wind is from Open-Meteo; water temperature and observed water level are from NOAA Tides & Currents. Predictions can't see storms or river flow. Not for navigation.
    Check CDFW before you go. Season dates shown here are the usual ones. The California Department of Fish and Wildlife can delay the opener or restrict crab traps (often to protect whales), and sets size limits, bag limits and licence rules.
